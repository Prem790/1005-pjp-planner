NOVEMBER CALENDAR AND ROUTES — v1

Inputs and purpose
The plan consumes the selected focus dealers, their commercial priorities, the
workbook officer start points, and its November working calendar. It uses only
the October 15 snapshot. No hidden simulation events or practice-seed outcomes
are used to construct this calendar.

Monthly obligations
November has 25 working days per officer (Monday–Saturday). Six dealer visits
per day produce 150 appointments per officer. All explicitly flagged clubs
receive two appointments, fourteen calendar days apart; non-clubs receive one.
The provisional focus sets therefore contain 142, 132 and 149 distinct dealers.
This follows the brief's explicit six-per-day requirement, above its approximate
100-account description. No dealer receives more than two appointments.

Date assignment
High-priority clubs get the earliest first-round slots, spread over the first
ten working days; their second round is exactly fourteen calendar days later.
Those commitments remain fixed during optional-appointment swaps.
Remote routes are seeded with a far dealer, then expanded using full-route
travel and geographic proximity. Remaining accounts start in commercial rank
order. A deterministic simulated-annealing search exchanges optional visits
between dates, minimizing travel and priority-weighted lateness. Overtime has
a steep penalty. Only a fully feasible candidate can be exported.

Heuristic parameters are in config/scheduling.json. The search is reproducible
with a fixed random seed. It is not a proof of the globally optimal monthly
calendar. Failure to find a calendar raises an error, preserving focus inputs;
it does not prove mathematical infeasibility. A bounded repair step can defer
an affected non-club dealer in a small remote cluster: direct travel exceeds
160 minutes and fewer than six currently selected known locations lie within
30 travel minutes of that dealer. It chooses the lowest commercial score in
those affected clusters, then the next ranked eligible reserve with an unused
owner root. All club commitments are protected. Every replacement is recorded
in focus_repairs.csv; the original selection outputs remain unchanged.
Final focus membership is exported as scheduled_focus_dealers.csv. Search
failure, rather than a proof that no possible route exists, motivates repairs.

Daily route order
For each proposed set of at most six accounts, an exact Held–Karp calculation
finds the shortest open route through the known locations from the officer's
fixed start. There is no return leg in the supplied simulator. Unknown-location
accounts have constant travel time and do not move the simulated position.
They are merged into the known route in descending priority without changing
its total travel time. This does not optimize the order of known dealers for
bad weather, and a nightly re-planner must decide which five to keep.

Time assumptions
A workday is 09:30–18:30. Six dealer services take 150 minutes (25 each).
Travel plus dealer service must fit within 460 minutes. The remaining eighty
minutes reserve two demand-generation calls, including travel: 17:10–17:50
and 17:50–18:30. No DG account identities or locations were supplied; these
are capacity reservations, not invented customer appointments. Routes that
finish early leave additional slack before the DG blocks. No lunch duration
is specified in the simulator; this baseline does not subtract a separate
lunch allowance. Production timing would need field agreement on those slots
and return-to-base requirements.

Travel assumptions
Haversine distance x 1.35 road factor; 18 km/h metro, 30 km/h upcountry.
Unknown locations use the simulator's 25/45-minute allowance. Missing GPS is
flagged per appointment: numerical feasibility under this model does not
establish real-world geographic feasibility. Route minutes are retained to
six decimals; displayed clock times round upwards to the next minute.

Weather diagnostic
Every day is initially planned as normal capacity. daily_routes.csv separately
checks whether its first five stops fit within 460 minutes at 1.5x travel time.
This is a stress test, not a forecast or evidence of 90% adherence. The sixth
stop needs cancellation or rescheduling on disrupted days even when the first
five fit. Unknown-location upcountry days can also overrun at five visits.
Unavailable dealers, sales changes and forecast-driven changes belong to the
next adaptive-planning milestone.

Outputs (artifacts/plan/, private and Git-ignored)
plan_november.csv: the full simulator-compatible plan, with explanations.
plan_SO-A.csv / plan_SO-B.csv / plan_SO-C.csv: required per-officer plans.
daily_routes.csv: time budgets, slack, DG blocks, GPS flags and rain diagnostic.
focus_repairs.csv: explicit replacement log, including priorities and reasons.
scheduled_focus_dealers.csv: final membership after the recorded repairs.
plan_summary.json / .txt: assumptions, configuration, hashes and territory totals.
verification.json: independent source/CSV reconciliation, generated separately.

Verification
python scripts/verify_plan.py --workbook data/private/PJP_Assignment_Sample_Data.xlsx
The verifier does not import the scheduling or routing implementation. It reads
the workbook and exports independently, checks the frequency of every focus
account and all club gaps, recalculates each leg, confirms daily budgets and
arrival times, and verifies that the source workbook remains unchanged.
