EVENING RE-PLANNER AND FINAL RESULTS

Contract and state
python -m pjp replan --state STATE.json --plan CURRENT_PLAN.csv --out NEXT_PLAN.csv
Requires the source-derived context generated by python -m pjp run. Memory
persists beside the simulator plan by default (one temporary directory per run),
or at an explicit --memory path. Never share memory across independent runs.
The evaluator uses separate memory for every practice seed and resets it before
re-running that seed. State dates must advance or repeat the current day;
observations on a repeated day overwrite that date instead of double-counting.

Available information
Only the supplied workbook snapshot, initial focus membership, current future
plan and evening state. The state provides observed sales and completed/missed
visits plus tomorrow's forecast. There is no seed parameter in the re-planner.
It imports neither the simulator nor its hidden world-generation code. Revealed
events are available only to evaluation diagnostics after the simulation.

Signals
Reference rate: signed sheets from July 16 to October 15 divided by historical
Monday–Saturday workdays. References below 0.5 sheets/workday do not have enough
support for these event triggers. For each observed working day, absent sales
entries are zero. Use 3–5 observed working days and an approximate negative-
binomial count likelihood with dispersion 2. Compare a drop-rate alternative
(quarter of baseline) or hike alternative (double baseline) against the normal
rate. This is a noisy-count heuristic, not the simulator's true event labels.

Require the three-day observed mean below 45% of baseline plus log evidence at
least 2.3 for drops. A repeat beyond an already-completed focus obligation needs
log evidence at least 4.0; weaker signals may only advance an owed visit. Hikes
need mean at least 1.9x reference, log evidence 2.7, and the last two observations
above baseline. Defer appropriate non-club visits seven calendar days, capped
before the month-end recovery period. Club obligations override hike holds.
Clear episodes when observations recover; do not repeatedly visit an account
for the same unresponsive episode. All thresholds are explicit in configuration.

Scheduling
Reconstruct remaining original-focus obligations from completed month-to-date
visits, not from what remains in the incoming plan alone. Thus a missed visit
or an earlier deferred row is not forgotten. Eligible drop requests may add a
second non-club visit, never a third. Preserve feasible existing anchors. Pull
late drop visits forward, subject to club spacing. Apply a five-visit cap and
1.5x/1.3x travel to forecast rain/traffic tomorrow; later days remain normal
capacity because no further forecasts are provided.

Remove lower-priority stops from over-budget routes, then insert missing tasks
into feasible future slots. Clubs rank above non-clubs, urgent supported drops
above routine visits, and missed/commercially important obligations receive
additional priority. An inserted task can displace a lower-priority task, which
returns to the queue. Exchanges are bounded; a lower-priority task cannot
oscillate by displacing a higher-priority one. Never silently erase unplaced
obligations: log them, including a separate protected-club shortfall list.
Every evening reconstructs still-owed obligations again.

Routing
Use the common exact open-path route solver. For tomorrow, enumerate at most
720 orders and choose a travel/priority compromise subject to the forecast time
budget, putting important visits earlier. Future days retain minimum travel
ordering. Unknown locations retain the simulator's fixed travel allowance and
unchanged known position. The whole output is checked for frequency and cadence
before atomic export. Separate independent verification checks every logged
future route without importing the solver or planner.

There are 24 evening calls per run (the final working day needs no future plan).
All 72 calls completed successfully, and independent verification checks more
than 16,000 future-plan rows. No club shortfalls were found in these future
plans. These results do not guarantee hidden-seed behaviour or future availability.

Trade-off
Do not present adherence improvement alone. Forecast false alarms reserve fewer
visits than actual clear weather would allow, reducing completed-call volume
and focus coverage. Protecting clubs and responding to drops reallocates which
non-club dealers get the remaining slots. The initial six-per-day calendar has
no explicit recovery buffer. The simulator rewards cancellations during hikes,
including moved appointments; this does not mean the business permanently
abandons those dealers. Original-date adherence falls when a visit moves even
if completed later. The static plan remains a useful alternative for a coverage-
first policy. Choosing between those business costs needs client input rather
than a claim that one policy dominates every metric.

Operational improvements
Reserve explicit recovery capacity in initial focus selection, calibrate forecast
costs, incorporate opening hours and dealer availability, obtain GPS and real
travel-time matrices, and model low-volume baseline uncertainty. Detecting a
quarter-demand episode from a few sparse observations is intrinsically hard.
Refresh draft talking points from live observations after re-planning. This POC
produces static examples as required and does not send actual WhatsApp messages.
